# Patchwork for iOS

An open-source native client for [Patchwork](https://github.com/nathanswavely/patchwork). **Early browsing prototype, not an App Store release.**

SwiftUI, iOS 17+, no third-party code dependencies (the only bundled third-party material is a set of Phosphor motif glyphs). Connects directly to an explicitly selected quilt using its public `/api/v1` endpoints. Nearly everything it does is a public read: no passwords, no analytics, no central proxy. You can sign in to a quilt by asking it to email you a six-digit code, and the writes the app makes are all yours and all on that one quilt: asking for the code, answering it, choosing a username the first time, signing in with a recovery code instead, signing out, following, joining, leaving or withdrawing a request on a patch, reading and clearing notifications, your own account settings — your profile, two preferences, recovery codes, your signed-in devices, your data, and deleting the account — adding an event to a patch: posting it where you are a member or an admin, or suggesting it for review where the patch takes suggestions, with the app saying which before you fill the form — and editing or deleting an event you may change: your own, where you are still a member, your own suggestion while it waits, or any on a patch you run — and taking part in a patch's governance: raising a proposal and withdrawing your own, nominating a member for admin, proposing a change to a governance document or to the rules, writing a new document on a patch you run, approving, declining or asking the members about a proposal where you are the maintainer and making an approved one official, voting on a proposal and changing the vote, casting an election's ballot, standing in an election or putting another member forward, and discussing a proposal by commenting, replying, editing or deleting your own comments, and reacting — and a patch's noticeboard where you are a member: putting up a notice, editing it, switching its replies on or off and taking it down, replying, removing a reply, and reporting a notice or a reply to the patch's admins — and the people side of an account: reporting a person to the quilt's admins, keeping a contact card and choosing which patches may read it, hiding a membership from your profile and the patch's public list, and choosing which notifications reach you in-app or by email. A session is a cookie the quilt sets, kept in the app's own cookie jar and sent only back to that quilt, so each saved quilt holds its own session and signing in to one tells no other quilt anything. A launch reads only a quilt you have chosen — on the first launch, the ones you pick; after that, the one you used last — and asks nothing authenticated of a quilt you have not signed in to.

## Run

Open `Patchwork.xcodeproj` in Xcode, choose the Patchwork scheme and an iPhone simulator, and Run. The project is checked in; XcodeGen is only needed after changing `project.yml` (`xcodegen generate --spec project.yml` from the repository root).

The app's identity is `com.swavelycreative.patchwork` on team `H886Z8M8DG`, version 0.1.0, build 1: raise `CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION` in `project.yml` for every upload. A fork builds under its own team and bundle identifier — change both in `project.yml` — and then cannot use passkeys on any quilt until that quilt lists the fork too (see Passkeys below).

Normal launches use live APIs. Add `--preview` to the scheme's launch arguments for clearly labeled fictional, offline design data in Debug builds. Preview connections are never persisted. Remove that argument to return to real quilts.

## Implemented path

1. The first launch opens on the featured quilt itself — live, pannable, its patches tappable — with the app's bars folded away under a welcome that gives the quilt's own description and size. **Start exploring** keeps that quilt, under its own name, and unfolds the app around the same canvas; **Find another quilt** opens the picker, where a quilt is found in the directory, among the featured quilt's neighbours, or by its HTTPS origin.
2. Every later launch opens straight onto the quilt used last, drawn at once from what the last launch kept (the whole tree, the description and the icon, in Caches) and read again behind the drawing; a quilt that cannot be reached stays drawn as it was last seen.
3. Explore the interactive quilt — it runs under the bars — pinch to zoom, pan, and filter by the quilt's most-worn tags to repack it. Switch to map or list from the round button in the bottom-right corner, which unfolds upward into Quilt · Map · List; all three read the same filter.
4. Search patches and upcoming events from the top bar's field (or the tab bar's Search button); one explicit row narrows the quilt to the query.
5. Tap a patch to dock its profile at the head's height; pull up for its four glimpses — About, Events, Members, Governance — and open any of them by its heading. Read the whole calendar and subscribe to it, the roster the patch publishes, and the documents, proposals and record it publishes. Open an event, get directions, use native sharing, or follow a link to its website. A patch that has moved opens read-only on the quilt it moved to.
6. Narrow the Events tab by date — today, tomorrow, this weekend, this week, next week, this month, or a custom range — resolved in the quilt's own time zone. An event's detail adds the flyer, the map and Maps directions, “with X” links, the host patch as a door, tickets on the source's own site, and Add to calendar.
7. Ask Discover what you're drawn to and get the patches wearing those tags, soonest event first.
8. Read how the quilt is run: the account menu's About this quilt opens a stack — About, The Label, The Lining, how governance works, the Privacy Policy and the User Agreement — which the end of List mode and Discover also reach by a quiet footer row.
9. Choose how it looks to you from the account menu's Display: Theme (System/Light/Dark) and Colors (Default/Muted). Both are held on the device and need no account.
10. Hold the Quilt tab (or use the account menu) to switch quilts; connected quilts appear as doorways. Saved quilts are local to the device; swipe one in the switcher to remove it. Find a quilt in the switcher adds another through the same inspect-and-confirm as before.
11. Sign in to a quilt from the account menu: give it your email, it sends a six-digit code, and you type the code (the link in the same email still works on the web). An address with no account yet is asked to choose a username. The menu then names you and offers Sign out, which ends that quilt's session and forgets its cookie. Each quilt is signed in to separately.
12. Follow or join a patch once you are signed in — from the heart on its card, or from the control in its profile's head: Follow a public patch, Join one that admits members (with an optional message where it approves them), and leave, unfollow or withdraw a request from the standing the head then wears. A Dashboard tab appears while you are signed in with what is asked of you, what is coming up, what you manage, what you are a member of, what you follow, and what you have asked for.
13. Read what the quilt needs you for: while you are signed in a badged bell sits beside the account menu, and the sheet behind it lists your notifications on cards — filtered by the web's own categories or to unread only, each row wearing its type's mark and how long ago it arrived. Tapping one marks it read and opens what it is about: the patch, its calendar, its roster, its governance, a proposal, a charter, an event, or a patch on a neighbour quilt — and the website for the surfaces this client has not built. Swipe to dismiss, mark all read, or clear the quilt's whole list behind a confirmation. The Dashboard says the same count in a row at its head.
14. Open Settings from the account menu: an index of sections, each its own page. Edit your display name, bio and links, and keep a contact card — phone, email, handle or note, each with an optional label — that nobody sees until you give it to a patch; choose which notifications reach you in-app and, where the quilt sends email, by email (the bell's ⋯ menu opens the same page); in My Patches, see every patch you claim, run, belong to, follow or have asked to join, and for one you run or belong to switch **Public** off — which hides you from your profile and the patch's public member list while its admins and members still see you — or choose which items on your card it can reach you by; choose to start on My Quilt (honoured once, at the next launch); hide patches with amended linings; make and read recovery codes and sign out other devices; take your personal export or a member seamrip through the share sheet; and, last and on its own page, delete your account — the quilt asks for a fresh proof first, which this app gives with a recovery code. The sign-in sheet also takes a username and a recovery code instead of an email.
15. Add an event to a patch from its calendar's bottom bar or under its profile's Events glimpse: **New event** where you are a member or an admin, **Suggest an event** where the patch takes suggestions from anybody signed in. The form is written in the patch's own time zone; a post opens its event page, and a suggestion ends on "Submitted for review" and waits for the patch's admins (or the quilt's, for a patch nobody runs). An event you may change carries **Edit** and **Delete** under its description — **Withdraw** for your own suggestion still waiting. Edit opens the same form, filled from the event and on its own clock, sends only what changed, and can clear a description, a location, an event page, a flyer or an end time; an edit that sends the event back to review ends on "Submitted for review". Delete asks first, then the page leaves and every list holding the event reads again. An event from a patch's calendar feed offers neither.
16. Take part in a patch's decisions where you are a member: its Governance room says how many proposals need your vote, and a proposal's screen holds the banner, the tally and quorum, the terms the vote keeps, and Approve · Reject · Abstain — the vote that is in filled, changeable with a tap, and a bar at the foot once the buttons have scrolled away. An election is approval voting: tick as many candidates as you like and save the whole ballot, or take part without approving anyone; while nominations are open, stand (with a statement) or put another member forward. Every proposal carries its discussion — comment, reply, edit or delete your own, and react with the six fixed emoji. A member or an admin raises a proposal from the bar at the foot of the Governance room and the proposals room — "New proposal", or "New change" for the admin of a patch whose admins decide — in a sheet that is what the patch's rules make it: a vote whose window starts on the patch's own default, a change applied on the spot or put to the members first as an advisory vote, a request sent to the maintainer, or, where the patch decides at meetings, a question opened for discussion with no ballot and no clock. It opens on its own screen once made, and its author can withdraw it from the banner while it is open. Where the community ratifies its admins, an admin's form also offers **Membership**, a nomination of a member picked from the roster. A document has its **History** — every version, each opening the change it made — and, for a member, **Propose change** (or **Change this document** for the admin of a patch whose admins decide): the text with Write / Preview, a count of the lines changed and the lines themselves, a summary and a reason, and the same outcome switch. "How decisions are made" carries **Propose a change to these rules** (or **Change these rules**): a native form of the rules members may change, set to what is in force, with what each comes to for this patch and every change listed current → proposed before it is sent. An amendment's screen shows what it changes. On a patch whose admins decide, the maintainer **Approves** or **Declines** a proposal from its banner at any moment it is open, or **Asks the members** first, once; on any patch, an admin **Makes official** a vote the members carried — each behind a confirmation. An admin writes a **New document** from the documents room, members only unless they choose otherwise. Publishing or unpublishing a document, recording a text a meeting adopted, and adding a council seat stay the website's.
17. Read and write on a patch's noticeboard where you are a member or an admin: the board newest first, each notice with who put it up, whether members were told and what its replies are doing; a notice's own screen with its one image, its Markdown body and its replies, oldest first; **Put up a notice** where the patch lets you, with Take replies and Tell members as the two switches; Reply while replies are on; Edit, the replies switch and Take down for the notice's author (the last two for a patch admin too); Remove on your own reply; and Report, which goes to the patch's admins. Nobody outside the room is shown a door, and a notice named by a notification opens here rather than on the website.
18. Open a person from wherever they are named with a username: a row in a patch's members room or its Members glimpse, an admin or a held council seat on the governance overview, a candidate on a slate or a voter on a ballot, the author of a notice or a reply (on the board, a press on the row), a steward on the Label, or your own from Settings' View public profile. Their profile says who they are and since when, where they have moved, how to reach them — the items they share with a patch you are also in, never naming which — their links, and the patches they run and belong to, each of which opens that patch. Your own profile has **Edit profile**; anybody else's has **Report** while you are signed in, which goes to the quilt's admins. Somebody the quilt no longer answers for reads "Person not found".
19. Keep a patch you run from your phone. The Dashboard's **Attention needed** lists only what is asked of you — pending member requests and suggested events on the patches you run, and proposals waiting on your vote — each a door to where it is answered, and the section is drawn only when one exists; the events coming up on your patches are a section of their own under it, **Upcoming events on your patches**. **Pending requests** shows each person (a door to their profile), when they asked and the note they wrote, in quotes, with **Approve** and **Reject** behind the web's "Are you sure?"; a `membership.request` notification opens the same queue. A patch's calendar heads its nights with **Suggested events** — when, where, what and who suggested it, the title opening the event — with **Approve**, which publishes it to everyone (edit it afterwards to keep it to followers or members), and **Decline**, a sheet that says the suggestion is deleted and offers a note for whoever suggested it. Your patch's Members room adds **Pending requests**, **Invite a member** (by username, with the invitations still out and **Rescind**) and **Removed members** (with **Reinstate**), and **Remove** on every member's row but your own, on the swipe and the long press — beside **Promote to admin** where the patch's rules let an admin make one (a maintainer's patch; never where admins are chosen elsewhere, ratified by proposal or elected, which the room's note explains in the server's terms) and **Make member** on an admin's row. Your patch's noticeboard opens with **Reports** and **Noticeboard settings**: the reports waiting, resolved and dismissed, each saying what was reported (a door to the notice), the reason and details and who reported it, with **Review** — dismiss it, remove what was reported (asked twice), or switch replies off on the notice, with a note — and the board's two settings, who puts up notices and whether new notices take replies; open reports are an ask on the Dashboard, and a `notice.reported` notification opens the queue. **Edit patch**, in the profile's ⋯, is the create form filled from the patch — name, description, address and its point on the map, timezone, website, image, links, tags, the tile, visibility and whether it takes event suggestions — and sends only what you changed. A patch's sources, notification categories, founded date, move to another quilt and archiving stay on the website, and the form links there.
20. Get in where the website let people in: answer a patch's invitation from the "You're invited" notification, where the patch's head offers **Accept** and **Decline** in the place Follow would be; **Add a patch** from the account menu or the Dashboard, which asks the web's "Is this your patch to run?" first — **I run this patch** is the create form (name, description, an address whose map point is only ever one you confirmed, website, tags and suggested tags, an optional tile with a live miniature, the lining, the membership policy preselected **Invite only**, and the four governance templates with **Minimal** the default), landing on the new patch as its admin; **Someone else runs it** is the suggestion form, also reached from an empty search or an empty list, which waits for an admin (or joins at once for a trusted contributor) and turns a name the quilt already holds into a door to that patch; claim a patch nobody runs from its profile or My Patches — the methods the listing takes, the records to copy, Verify Now, Resend, Withdraw, the emailed link said plainly to be the browser's and picked up when the app comes back — and set it up once approved, in the same form; read what creating an account agrees to on the username step, and be welcomed once afterwards; and, signed out, paste an account invite link for this quilt to make an account with it.

Lancaster is the directory's only entry, and so the featured quilt a first launch opens onto; that first launch reads it before anybody has chosen it. Direct connection does not depend on directory inclusion. HTTPS root origins only in this prototype; subpath hosting, invitations, registry URLs, QR codes, directory administration, and location discovery are not implemented.

Textile decoration and display fonts are intentionally omitted. Reduce Motion disables rearrangement animation; the list provides full text at accessibility sizes.

## API boundary

Reads `instance` (including `stats`, `submissions_enabled`, and `geography.timezone`, the zone the date presets resolve in), `instance/icon`, `instance/lining`, `nodes/tree` (including each patch's `appearance`), `nodes/{slug}` (node plus the `is_unclaimed` and `lining_status` envelope), `tags` (for tag motifs), `label`, `legal/{doc}` for `privacy` and `terms`, `events` (including `node_slug`, `after`, `limit`, `from`, `to`, and `include_past` — `from`/`to` always travel as instants, never bare dates, because the server compares `starts_at` as text), `events/{id}` (which adds `visibility`, `node_status`, `node_id`, `latitude`/`longitude`, `recurrence`, `image_url`/`image_alt`, `status`, `source_id`, `links[]` and `mentions[]`), and `events/{id}/event.ics`. No `scope=my` — every one of those reads is the public quilt, signed in or not.

Sign-in adds the only calls that are not reads: `POST auth/magic-link` (`{email}`), `POST auth/magic-link/verify` (`{email, code}`, which answers with either the user or `{"status":"username_required","signup_token"}`), `POST auth/signup` (`{token, username, display_name}`), `GET auth/me`, and `POST auth/logout`. Every non-GET sends `X-Patchwork-Request: true` and `Content-Type: application/json`, which the server requires. The session is the quilt's own `patchwork_session` cookie — there are no bearer tokens — kept in the app's `HTTPCookieStorage` so it survives a relaunch. Cookies are host-scoped, so each saved quilt holds its own session and no quilt is sent another's; `auth/me` is asked only where that host already has such a cookie, so a reader who has not signed in makes no authenticated request at all. Signing out posts `auth/logout` and then deletes that host's cookies.

Following and joining add the rest of the writes: `GET me/nodes` (the reader's own memberships, active and pending), `POST nodes/{slug}/join` with `{"role":"follower"}` to follow and with `{}` or `{"message"}` to join — which answers `active` or `pending` according to the patch's `membership_policy` — `POST nodes/{slug}/leave` for both Unfollow and Leave, `POST nodes/{slug}/withdraw` for a request nobody has answered yet, and `GET events?scope=my` for what is coming up on the patches the reader holds a row on. `GET nodes/{slug}` carries `is_member`, `is_admin`, `membership_role`, `is_banned` and `membership_policy` alongside the public node where there is a session, and the Dashboard's badges read `nodes/{slug}/members?status=pending` and `nodes/{slug}/proposals?status=open` for the patches the reader admins or belongs to. Every one of these is asked only while signed in; a signed-out launch makes exactly the public reads it always did, and a 401 from any of them means the session is gone.

The bell adds the last of the writes: `GET notifications` (with `limit`, `after`, `unread=true` and `category=proposals|governance|membership|events|moderation`), `GET notifications/count`, `PATCH notifications/{id}/read`, `POST notifications/read-all`, `DELETE notifications/{id}` and `DELETE notifications`, which clears the quilt's whole list rather than the page in view. A row is unread exactly when it carries no `read_at`. `PATCH` and `DELETE` are the only verbs beyond `GET` and `POST` this client sends, and they carry the same `X-Patchwork-Request` header and JSON content type every other write does. The count is read once when a session is confirmed, every sixty seconds while a signed-in reader has the app in front of them, and again whenever the app returns to the foreground; it is never asked for without a session and is cleared by signing out.

The patch profile's rooms add `nodes/{slug}/members` (with `after`), `nodes/{slug}/governance`, `governance/{id}`, `nodes/{slug}/governance/overview`, `nodes/{slug}/governance/record`, `nodes/{slug}/proposals` (with `status`), and `proposals/{id}`. `nodes/{slug}/events.ics` and `nodes/{slug}/events.rss` are handed to the reader's own calendar or feed app rather than fetched. A patch on a connected quilt is read from that quilt's own `nodes/{slug}`, `events`, `instance`, and `instance/icon`.

Events add one write: `POST events` with `node_id`, `title`, `starts_at` and, only where they are filled, `description`, `location`, `ends_at`, `event_url`, `image_url` and `image_alt` — trimmed, as instants, with no `timezone` (the event inherits its patch's) and no `recurrence` (the server refuses one) — and `visibility` only when the reader is posting directly. It answers 201 with the event, whose `status` is `active` or `pending_review`. The door reads `nodes/{slug}`'s `timezone`, `accept_event_suggestions`, `follower_permissions.events` and `moved_to`, the envelope's `viewer_trusted` and `is_banned`, the membership index, `auth/me`'s `role`, and `instance`'s `submissions_enabled`. A refusal is shown in the server's own words; a moved patch's 403 carries `moved_to` beside its sentence, which `APIError` keeps. A pending suggestion is readable by its submitter at `events/{id}` and is on no list, so the app lists it nowhere.

Editing adds two more: `PATCH events/{id}` with only the keys that changed — `title`, `description`, `location`, `starts_at`, `ends_at`, `event_url`, `image_url`, `image_alt`, and `visibility` only through the direct door — where `""` clears a text field and `ends_at: null` clears the end (the web cannot clear any of them); never `timezone`, `recurrence` or `node_id`. It answers 200 with the bare event, whose `status` is `pending_review` when the edit went back into review, and the detail reads `events/{id}` again. `DELETE events/{id}` removes the row. The server sends no `may_edit`: the app works the right out from `events/{id}`'s `created_by`, `node_status`, `status`, `source_id` and `viewer_trusted`, the membership index and `auth/me`'s `role`, as the web does.

Proposing adds two writes. `POST nodes/{slug}/proposals` takes `{"title","body","proposal_type"}` — `action` or `other` — with `duration_hours` only where a vote will run and `put_to_vote` only from an admin of an admin-decides patch; it answers 201 with the bare proposal, whose id the new screen reads the rest by. The state it is born in is the server's (`in_effect`, `elsewhere`, `awaiting_admin` or `voting`, in that order), and the form ports that switch so it says what will happen before it is sent. The server sets no length limit on a proposal, so only the title is checked first. `DELETE proposals/{id}` withdraws; the server takes it from the author or a patch admin and refuses a closed proposal or an election, and the app offers it to the author alone, as the web does. The reader's standing is the membership index's, never `is_admin`, which a quilt admin with no role also carries and whom the server refuses.

Governance authoring adds the rest of the acts. `POST proposals/{id}/decide` (`{"decision":"approve"|"decline"}`), `POST proposals/{id}/open-vote` (an empty body: the proposal's own default window) and `POST proposals/{id}/apply`, each followed by `proposals/{id}` read again; the gate is the detail's `can_decide` and the membership index, and `current_doc_content`, `proposed_body`, `proposed_title`, `doc_text_hidden`, `ground_moved`, `ground_moved_at`, `applied_at` and `target_user_name` are now read from it. A nomination is `POST nodes/{slug}/proposals` with `proposal_type:"membership"` and `target_user_id`. An amendment is the same call with `proposal_type:"amendment"`, `target_doc` (the document's `filename`, now read from `governance/{id}` with its `node_id`), the whole `proposed_body`, `proposed_title` and `change_summary`, and `duration_hours`/`put_to_vote` where the outcome asks for them; a rules change sends `target_doc:"governance-rules.json"` and the whole rules file as indented JSON, read first from `GET nodes/{slug}/governance/rules` (every field; its keys put back into snake case after the app's decoder camel-cases them) beside `GET nodes/{slug}/governance/electorate` (`members`, `tenure_days`, `patch_age_days`; members and admins only, best effort). History is `GET governance/{id}/versions` and `GET governance/{id}/diff?from=&to=` (word-level `hunks`; the server sends no whole texts). A new document is `POST nodes/{slug}/governance` with `{title, body, visibility}`, trimmed, visibility always sent; it answers 201 with the document.

Governance adds the member's acts: `POST proposals/{id}/vote` (`{"value"}`, and the proposal read again after it, because a sole voter's ballot settles it in the request), `PUT proposals/{id}/ballot` (`{"candidate_ids","abstain"}` — the whole set each time, candidate row ids), `POST proposals/{id}/candidates` (`{"statement"}` to stand, `{"user_id"}` to put somebody forward), `DELETE proposals/{id}/candidates/me`, `GET`/`POST proposals/{id}/comments` (`{"body","parent_id"}`), `PATCH`/`DELETE comments/{id}`, `POST comments/{id}/reactions` (`{"emoji"}`) and `DELETE comments/{id}/reactions/{emoji}`, the emoji percent-encoded in the path and the heart always sent with its variation selector. `PUT` joins the verbs. The nominee picker pages `nodes/{slug}/members` by `after`. `proposals/{id}` is read with the session, which is what fills `can_vote`, `my_vote`, `approved_by_me`, `i_abstained` and every reaction's `me`; the overview's `needs_vote` is the room's count. Who may comment is the membership index plus the patch's `follower_permissions.proposals`, read from `nodes/{slug}` for a follower before a composer is drawn.

The noticeboard adds a member's reads and writes, every one of which answers 404 to anybody outside the room: `GET nodes/{slug}/notices` (`limit`, `after`; newest first, with `may_post`, `replies_default` and `notice_posting` beside the page), `POST nodes/{slug}/notices` (`{"title","body","image_url","image_alt","replies_open","tell_members"}`), `GET notices/{id}` (the notice with `node_slug`, `may_edit` and `may_manage`), `PATCH notices/{id}` — sent either as an edit (the four content fields, the author's) or as the replies switch alone (`{"replies_open"}`, the author's or a patch admin's), never both, because the server refuses the pair whole for an admin who is not the author — `DELETE notices/{id}`, `GET`/`POST notices/{id}/replies` (oldest first; `{"body"}`), `DELETE replies/{id}`, and `POST reports` (`{"entity_type":"notice"|"reply","entity_id","reason","details"}`). Lengths are checked in bytes before sending, as the server counts them.

Settings adds the account's own calls: `PATCH auth/me` with only the fields being saved (`display_name`, `bio`, `links`, `start_on_my_quilt`, `hide_amended_linings`), `GET`/`POST auth/recovery-codes` (counts, and a new batch of ten shown once), `GET auth/sessions`, `POST auth/sessions/revoke-others`, `DELETE auth/sessions/{id}`, `GET users/me/export` and `GET users/me/seamrip` (named by their `Content-Disposition` and handed to the share sheet), and `DELETE users/me` with `{"confirm_username"}`. That last one is step-up gated: a 403 whose `code` is `sudo_required` or `passkey_required` opens the Confirm sheet, which reads `GET auth/step-up` (`recovery_ready`) and spends a code with `POST auth/step-up/recovery`, then the call is made once more. A 409 names `sole_admin` (with the patches in the way) or `last_instance_admin`. `POST auth/recovery` (`{username, code}`) signs in with a code and opens the step-up window on arrival. `APIError` now keeps a refusal's `code` and `patches` beside its sentence. Start on My Quilt is read once per launch from `auth/me` and never re-asserted (web ADR 035).

People add one read and the account's last writes. `GET users/{username}` is sign-in optional and answers the same for everybody — identity, `links`, `moved_to`, `created_at` and `memberships` (active, visible `admin`/`member` rows on public, active patches only) — except `contact`, which is filled only for a signed-in reader who is an active member or admin of a patch the person shared those items into, and is `[]` otherwise. It answers 404 alike for an unknown, suspended or deleted account. Reporting a person is the noticeboard's `POST reports` with `entity_type: "user"` and `entity_id` set to the profile's `id` (never the username); it goes to the instance admins' queue. No door is built from a blank username: a deleted account, a hidden member and a withheld chair all arrive with one. The card is `GET`/`POST users/me/contact-items` (`{kind, value, label}`; `shared_with` counts the patches an item is shared into), `PATCH`/`DELETE users/me/contact-items/{id}` and `DELETE users/me/contact-items/{id}/shares`; sharing is `GET nodes/{slug}/contact-shares` and `PUT nodes/{slug}/contact-shares` with `{"item_ids"}`, the whole set replaced. Visibility is `PATCH users/me/memberships/{node_id}` with `{"visible"}`. My Patches also reads `GET me/nodes?status=archived` and `GET users/me/claims`, and `contact_items_shared` on the membership index. Preferences are `GET`/`PUT notifications/preferences`; the `PUT` carries `{"preferences":[{type, channel, enabled}]}` with only the pairs the reader changed, because any row the server stores marks its type decided and re-sending the whole matrix would retire Discover's one-time bulletin offer. Each type's `channels` is an object keyed by channel name, and the snake-case decoder rewrites those keys too (`in_app` → `inApp`), so the map is matched back against the `channels` list.

A patch's admin adds the queues. `GET nodes/{slug}/members?status=pending` (paged by `after`; `join_message` only on this listing, `joined_at` the moment of asking, `member_count` the whole queue) and `?status=banned` are honoured only for the patch's admins — anybody else is quietly given the active list, not a refusal — so every door to them is drawn from the membership index's admin standing, never `is_admin`, and each screen checks it again. `PATCH nodes/{slug}/members/{userId}` (the user's id) takes `{"status":"active"}` to approve, `{"status":"left"}` to reject a request or reinstate a removed member, and `{"status":"banned"}` to remove; the same path takes `{"role":"admin"}` or `{"role":"member"}`, and never `follower`. The listing's `invited` key (patch admins only) is the Invited list; `POST nodes/{slug}/invitations` takes `{"username"}` as typed and trimmed, and `DELETE nodes/{slug}/invitations/{userId}` rescinds — both 404 to a quilt admin with no role here. `GET nodes/{slug}/event-submissions` is unpaged and arrives with `timezone`, the image pair and `event_url` blank, so a suggestion's time is read in the patch's own zone and the event is handed to its page carrying it; `PATCH events/{id}/review` takes `{"action":"approve"}` or `{"action":"reject","note"}`, the note only where one was written. The proposals list's `awaiting_your_vote` is read for the Dashboard. None of these is step-up gated, and every refusal is shown in the server's own sentence.

The rest of Patch Settings adds four calls. `GET nodes/{slug}/reports?status=pending|resolved|dismissed` (paged by `after`, oldest first, no total; each row quotes its `target`, names its `notice_id` and `reporter_name`, and says `gone`) and `PATCH nodes/{slug}/reports/{id}` with `{"action":"dismiss"|"remove"|"close_replies","resolution_note"}` (the note always sent, trimmed) answer only the patch's own admin — 404 to a quilt admin with no role, like the room — and refuse a second review with "this report has already been reviewed". The board's settings are `PATCH nodes/{slug}` with `{"notice_posting":"members"|"admins"}` or `{"notice_replies_default":bool}`, one field per save as the web sends them; the board's `notice_posting` and `replies_default` are read back from `GET nodes/{slug}/notices`. A role change reads `GET nodes/{slug}/governance/overview` first: Promote to admin is offered only where `leadership_venue` is not `elsewhere` and `leadership_model` is neither `meritocratic` nor `elected`, because the server answers each of those 409 with a sentence about the patch's own mechanism; the last-admin floor ("cannot demote the last admin") is the server's to say. Editing the patch is `PATCH nodes/{slug}` with only the fields that changed: text fields trimmed, `""` to clear; `latitude`/`longitude` as numbers, or both `null` to take the patch off the map; `tags` whole, with `suggest_tags` when a word was suggested; `appearance` (`palette`, `block`, `rotation`, `icon`) only when the tile changed and never over a drafted block or a bundle; `visibility`, `accept_event_suggestions`, `timezone` (`""` follows the quilt), `image_url` with `image_alt` as a pair, and `links` whole. A zone change that moves events is answered 409 `timezone_events_undecided` with the server's sentence; the app asks it and sends the same body again with `timezone_events:"keep_clock"|"keep_instant"`.

Getting in adds the reader's way onto a quilt and into a patch. `GET users/me/invitations` (`{"items":[{id,node_slug,node_name,status:"invited",invited_at}]}`, read beside `me/nodes` and never merged into it, because an invited row is not membership) and `POST nodes/{slug}/invitations/accept` (`{status:"active"}`) and `…/decline` (`{status:"declined"}`). `POST nodes` with `name`, `visibility:"public"`, `membership_policy` (always — an omitted one is `open` on the server), `template`, `appearance` (`palette`, `block`, `rotation`, and `icon` only when chosen), and only where filled `description`, `address`, `website`, `latitude`/`longitude` (only a point the reader confirmed on the map), `tags` and `suggest_tags`; it answers 201 with the node and, where a suggested word was declined before, `tag_warning`. `GET gazetteer/suggest?q=` (signed in, three characters or more) proposes a point; no address is sent to any geocoder but the quilt's own. `POST submissions` with `name`, `description` (always), and where filled `website`, `links` (`{url,label}`, a row with no address dropped), `address`, `feed_url` and `tags`; it answers `{status:"unclaimed",node}` or `{status:"pending_review",message,id}`, and a 409 carries `existing_slug`, which `Refusal` now keeps. Claiming is `GET nodes/{slug}/claims/mine` (the open claim, `methods` keyed by method — the snake-case decoder rewrites `meta_tag` — `verification_domain`, `node_status`), `POST nodes/{slug}/claim` (`{method, evidence?, email?}`, only the field the method asks for), `POST claims/{id}/verify` (200 either way: `verified`, or the server's `error` sentence), `POST claims/{id}/resend-email`, `POST claims/{id}/withdraw`, `POST claims/{id}/setup` (`{template, membership_policy}`; 410 and 409 said in the server's and the web's words), then `PATCH nodes/{slug}` with the form's fields (a failure there is not fatal), and `GET users/me/claims` again whenever the app returns to the front. An account invite is `GET auth/invite/{token}/validate` and `POST auth/invite` (`{token, username, display_name?, email?}` — email only where `instance.email_enabled`), which sets the session cookie like a sign-in; `auth/recovery-codes` follows it on a quilt without mail. `instance` now also reads `geography.latitude`/`longitude` and `email_enabled`, and `auth/me` `trusted_contributor`. Still the website's: sending and rescinding invitations (another slice), generating invite links, reviewing claims and vetting domains, the block drafter and fabric wall, template previews, the first account's bootstrap token, and opening any of these links in the app (no associated domains).

Every one of the endpoints above the sign-in paragraph is a public read, and the app reads them the same way whether or not anyone is signed in: it asks for no member-only view of a patch, so a patch's own disclosure settings — `public_member_list`, `public_governance_record`, `visibility`, and the server's `published_only` / `admins_withheld` / `proposals_withheld` flags — are honoured as the server states them, and a withheld list is never rendered as an empty one. DTOs decode only fields this client uses; unknown fields are tolerated. The app's cookie jar is its own — no web view shares it, and nothing is forwarded from one quilt to another. Each selected quilt gets a new navigation subtree; old quilt content is discarded.

The client's direct visit to a selected quilt is not a cross-quilt blended read. It does not use `multi_quilt: false` as permission to blend data from other instances.

Following, joining, leaving and withdrawing are implemented natively; so are proposing, withdrawing your own proposal, voting, an election's ballot and candidacy, and a proposal's discussion; so are nominating, amending a document and the rules, a document's history, a new document, and deciding, asking the members about and making official a proposal; so is a patch's noticeboard for its members — reading it, putting up and managing a notice, replying and reporting; so are posting an event to a patch, suggesting one, and editing, deleting or withdrawing one you may change; signing in by emailed code is implemented; reading, dismissing and clearing notifications is implemented; account settings and deletion are implemented, with recovery codes as the proof of presence; so are a patch admin's queues — answering requests to join, reviewing suggested events, inviting by username and rescinding, removing a member and reinstating them, making a member an admin and an admin a member, the noticeboard's report queue and its settings, and editing the patch's details and tile; so are people's profiles, reporting a person, the contact card and what each patch may read of it, membership visibility, and notification preferences; so are passkeys, but only on the quilts listed under Passkeys below, because a passkey belongs to its domain and iOS lets an app use one only where both sides vouch for each other. Moving a patch, the activity feed (held back pending nathanswavely/patchwork#398), the personal calendar feed, the steward listing, generating account invite links, editing a reply, member-only content, a patch's sources, notification categories, verification, founded date, move and archiving, moving someone to follower, event links, the trust request, the CSV upload, detaching an imported event and the any-patch event form, publishing or unpublishing a document, recording a text a meeting adopted, adding a council seat, revising a proposal, and moderation are follow-on work. The web remains the explicit route to those. No disabled pretend buttons represent them, and no control appears for a reader who is not signed in.

## Passkeys

A passkey is bound to the quilt's domain, and iOS lets this app use one only where the app's entitlements name that domain (`webcredentials:<domain>`) *and* the quilt's own `/.well-known/apple-app-site-association` lists this app — which a quilt's admin does under Administration → Settings → Apps by entering `H886Z8M8DG.com.swavelycreative.patchwork` (web ADR 2026-09-20). Where both hold, the sign-in sheet offers **Sign in with a passkey** (username-less: the system offers whichever passkey the device holds for that quilt), the Confirm sheet leads with **Confirm with your passkey** for an account that has one, and Settings → Security lists the account's passkeys with Add a passkey, rename and remove. Everywhere else none of that is drawn: the emailed code signs in, a recovery code confirms, and Security says in one line why there is no passkey here.

The calls are `POST auth/webauthn/login/begin` and `/finish`, `POST auth/webauthn/register/begin` and `/finish` (with the passkey's `name`), `POST auth/step-up/begin` and `/finish`, and `GET`/`PATCH`/`DELETE auth/credentials[/{id}]`. The finish bodies are the web's `webauthn.js` shapes — `id`, `rawId`, `type`, `response.{clientDataJSON, authenticatorData, signature, userHandle}` or `response.{clientDataJSON, attestationObject}` — every binary field base64url without padding, sent as spelled (never through the snake-case encoder every other write uses). Login finish sets the same `patchwork_session` cookie an emailed code does. Removing a passkey also signs out the account's other devices (the server's rule), and the confirmation says so.

**Quilts this app uses passkeys on:** `lancasterpatchwork.org`.

**Criteria for adding a quilt** (the ADR asks every app to publish its own): the quilt's admin asks for it; the quilt is served over HTTPS at a stable domain with its own admin, not a test or preview host; the admin lists `H886Z8M8DG.com.swavelycreative.patchwork` under Apps and the file is live at `/.well-known/apple-app-site-association`; and the domain is added in one release to both `project.yml` (the `associated-domains` entitlement) and `PasskeyDomains.all`, which a unit test keeps in step. Removing a quilt is the same change in reverse. A quilt is never added because it is large or popular, and an app built from this source under another team must be listed by each quilt itself.

## Testing

Two test plans, checked in beside the project:

- **Smoke** (`Smoke.xctestplan`, the default, so ⌘U runs it): every unit test, plus the seven paths that must never break — first launch to the quilt, a patch's rooms and an event and switching quilts; signing in by code and out; following from a card and letting go from the Dashboard; a member posting an event; voting and changing the vote; saving a profile and the next launch honouring it; putting up a notice and taking it down. A few minutes. It runs on every pull request.
- **Full** (`Full.xctestplan`): every test, the accessibility audits included, with the UI tests spread across parallel simulators. It runs on every merge to main and before any release beyond internal TestFlight.

While building, run only the classes a change touches (`-only-testing:PatchworkUITests/<Class>`). The tiers on Xcode Cloud: a pull-request workflow that runs Smoke, and a main workflow that runs Full and then archives for TestFlight, so every build testers receive has passed everything.

```sh
xcodebuild test -project Patchwork.xcodeproj -scheme Patchwork -testPlan Smoke \
  -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 17'
xcodebuild test -project Patchwork.xcodeproj -scheme Patchwork -testPlan Full \
  -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=iPhone 17' -parallel-testing-enabled YES
```
