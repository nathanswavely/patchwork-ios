# Patchwork for iOS

An open-source native client for [Patchwork](https://github.com/nathanswavely/patchwork). **Early browsing prototype, not an App Store release.**

SwiftUI, iOS 17+, no third-party code dependencies (the only bundled third-party material is a set of Phosphor motif glyphs). Connects directly to an explicitly selected quilt using its public `/api/v1` endpoints. Nearly everything it does is a public read: no passwords, no analytics, no central proxy. You can sign in to a quilt by asking it to email you a six-digit code, and the writes the app makes are all yours and all on that one quilt: asking for the code, answering it, choosing a username the first time, signing in with a recovery code instead, signing out, following, joining, leaving or withdrawing a request on a patch, reading and clearing notifications, your own account settings — your profile, two preferences, recovery codes, your signed-in devices, your data, and deleting the account — adding an event to a patch: posting it where you are a member or an admin, or suggesting it for review where the patch takes suggestions, with the app saying which before you fill the form — and taking part in a patch's governance: voting on a proposal and changing the vote, casting an election's ballot, standing in an election or putting another member forward, and discussing a proposal by commenting, replying, editing or deleting your own comments, and reacting — and a patch's noticeboard where you are a member: putting up a notice, editing it, switching its replies on or off and taking it down, replying, removing a reply, and reporting a notice or a reply to the patch's admins. A session is a cookie the quilt sets, kept in the app's own cookie jar and sent only back to that quilt, so each saved quilt holds its own session and signing in to one tells no other quilt anything. A launch reads only the quilt you explicitly inspect or select, and asks nothing authenticated of a quilt you have not signed in to.

## Run

Open `Patchwork.xcodeproj` in Xcode, choose the Patchwork scheme and an iPhone simulator, and Run. The project is checked in; XcodeGen is only needed after changing `project.yml` (`xcodegen generate --spec project.yml` from the repository root).

For an actual device, choose your development team and a bundle identifier in Signing & Capabilities. The prototype identifier is not an App Store identity.

Normal launches use live APIs. Add `--preview` to the scheme's launch arguments for clearly labeled fictional, offline design data in Debug builds. Preview connections are never persisted. Remove that argument to return to real quilts.

## Implemented path

1. Choose a quilt from the small starter directory or enter its HTTPS origin.
2. Inspect the returned quilt identity and explicitly select Explore quilt.
3. Explore the interactive quilt — it runs under the bars — pinch to zoom, pan, and filter by the quilt's most-worn tags to repack it. Switch to map or list from the floating control; all three read the same filter.
4. Search patches and upcoming events from the top bar's field (or the tab bar's Search button); one explicit row narrows the quilt to the query.
5. Tap a patch to dock its profile at the head's height; pull up for its four glimpses — About, Events, Members, Governance — and open any of them by its heading. Read the whole calendar and subscribe to it, the roster the patch publishes, and the documents, proposals and record it publishes. Open an event, get directions, use native sharing, or follow a link to its website. A patch that has moved opens read-only on the quilt it moved to.
6. Narrow the Events tab by date — today, tomorrow, this weekend, this week, next week, this month, or a custom range — resolved in the quilt's own time zone. An event's detail adds the flyer, the map and Maps directions, “with X” links, the host patch as a door, tickets on the source's own site, and Add to calendar.
7. Ask Discover what you're drawn to and get the patches wearing those tags, soonest event first.
8. Read how the quilt is run: the account menu's About this quilt opens a stack — About, The Label, The Lining, how governance works, the Privacy Policy and the User Agreement — which the end of List mode and Discover also reach by a quiet footer row.
9. Choose how it looks to you from the account menu's Display: Theme (System/Light/Dark) and Colors (Default/Muted). Both are held on the device and need no account.
10. Hold the Quilt tab (or use the account menu) to switch quilts; connected quilts appear as doorways. Saved quilts are local to the device; no quilt is preselected at launch.
11. Sign in to a quilt from the account menu: give it your email, it sends a six-digit code, and you type the code (the link in the same email still works on the web). An address with no account yet is asked to choose a username. The menu then names you and offers Sign out, which ends that quilt's session and forgets its cookie. Each quilt is signed in to separately.
12. Follow or join a patch once you are signed in — from the heart on its card, or from the control in its profile's head: Follow a public patch, Join one that admits members (with an optional message where it approves them), and leave, unfollow or withdraw a request from the standing the head then wears. A Dashboard tab appears while you are signed in with what needs you, what you manage, what you are a member of, what you follow, and what you have asked for.
13. Read what the quilt needs you for: while you are signed in a badged bell sits beside the account menu, and the sheet behind it lists your notifications on cards — filtered by the web's own categories or to unread only, each row wearing its type's mark and how long ago it arrived. Tapping one marks it read and opens what it is about: the patch, its calendar, its roster, its governance, a proposal, a charter, an event, or a patch on a neighbour quilt — and the website for the surfaces this client has not built. Swipe to dismiss, mark all read, or clear the quilt's whole list behind a confirmation. The Dashboard says the same count in a row at its head.
14. Open Settings from the account menu: an index of sections, each its own page. Edit your display name, bio and links; choose to start on My Quilt (honoured once, at the next launch); hide patches with amended linings; make and read recovery codes and sign out other devices; take your personal export or a member seamrip through the share sheet; and, last and on its own page, delete your account — the quilt asks for a fresh proof first, which this app gives with a recovery code. The sign-in sheet also takes a username and a recovery code instead of an email.
15. Add an event to a patch from its calendar's bottom bar or under its profile's Events glimpse: **New event** where you are a member or an admin, **Suggest an event** where the patch takes suggestions from anybody signed in. The form is written in the patch's own time zone; a post opens its event page, and a suggestion ends on "Submitted for review" and waits for the patch's admins (or the quilt's, for a patch nobody runs).
16. Take part in a patch's decisions where you are a member: its Governance room says how many proposals need your vote, and a proposal's screen holds the banner, the tally and quorum, the terms the vote keeps, and Approve · Reject · Abstain — the vote that is in filled, changeable with a tap, and a bar at the foot once the buttons have scrolled away. An election is approval voting: tick as many candidates as you like and save the whole ballot, or take part without approving anyone; while nominations are open, stand (with a statement) or put another member forward. Every proposal carries its discussion — comment, reply, edit or delete your own, and react with the six fixed emoji. Proposing, withdrawing and deciding stay the website's.
17. Read and write on a patch's noticeboard where you are a member or an admin: the board newest first, each notice with who put it up, whether members were told and what its replies are doing; a notice's own screen with its one image, its Markdown body and its replies, oldest first; **Put up a notice** where the patch lets you, with Take replies and Tell members as the two switches; Reply while replies are on; Edit, the replies switch and Take down for the notice's author (the last two for a patch admin too); Remove on your own reply; and Report, which goes to the patch's admins. Nobody outside the room is shown a door, and a notice named by a notification opens here rather than on the website.

Lancaster is one directory entry, not the default. Direct connection does not depend on directory inclusion. HTTPS root origins only in this prototype; subpath hosting, invitations, registry URLs, QR codes, directory administration, and location discovery are not implemented.

## API boundary

Reads `instance` (including `stats`, `submissions_enabled`, and `geography.timezone`, the zone the date presets resolve in), `instance/icon`, `instance/lining`, `nodes/tree` (including each patch's `appearance`), `nodes/{slug}` (node plus the `is_unclaimed` and `lining_status` envelope), `tags` (for tag motifs), `label`, `legal/{doc}` for `privacy` and `terms`, `events` (including `node_slug`, `after`, `limit`, `from`, `to`, and `include_past` — `from`/`to` always travel as instants, never bare dates, because the server compares `starts_at` as text), `events/{id}` (which adds `visibility`, `node_status`, `node_id`, `latitude`/`longitude`, `recurrence`, `image_url`/`image_alt`, `status`, `source_id`, `links[]` and `mentions[]`), and `events/{id}/event.ics`. No `scope=my` — every one of those reads is the public quilt, signed in or not.

Sign-in adds the only calls that are not reads: `POST auth/magic-link` (`{email}`), `POST auth/magic-link/verify` (`{email, code}`, which answers with either the user or `{"status":"username_required","signup_token"}`), `POST auth/signup` (`{token, username, display_name}`), `GET auth/me`, and `POST auth/logout`. Every non-GET sends `X-Patchwork-Request: true` and `Content-Type: application/json`, which the server requires. The session is the quilt's own `patchwork_session` cookie — there are no bearer tokens — kept in the app's `HTTPCookieStorage` so it survives a relaunch. Cookies are host-scoped, so each saved quilt holds its own session and no quilt is sent another's; `auth/me` is asked only where that host already has such a cookie, so a reader who has not signed in makes no authenticated request at all. Signing out posts `auth/logout` and then deletes that host's cookies.

Following and joining add the rest of the writes: `GET me/nodes` (the reader's own memberships, active and pending), `POST nodes/{slug}/join` with `{"role":"follower"}` to follow and with `{}` or `{"message"}` to join — which answers `active` or `pending` according to the patch's `membership_policy` — `POST nodes/{slug}/leave` for both Unfollow and Leave, `POST nodes/{slug}/withdraw` for a request nobody has answered yet, and `GET events?scope=my` for what is coming up on the patches the reader holds a row on. `GET nodes/{slug}` carries `is_member`, `is_admin`, `membership_role`, `is_banned` and `membership_policy` alongside the public node where there is a session, and the Dashboard's badges read `nodes/{slug}/members?status=pending` and `nodes/{slug}/proposals?status=open` for the patches the reader admins or belongs to. Every one of these is asked only while signed in; a signed-out launch makes exactly the public reads it always did, and a 401 from any of them means the session is gone.

The bell adds the last of the writes: `GET notifications` (with `limit`, `after`, `unread=true` and `category=proposals|governance|membership|events|moderation`), `GET notifications/count`, `PATCH notifications/{id}/read`, `POST notifications/read-all`, `DELETE notifications/{id}` and `DELETE notifications`, which clears the quilt's whole list rather than the page in view. A row is unread exactly when it carries no `read_at`. `PATCH` and `DELETE` are the only verbs beyond `GET` and `POST` this client sends, and they carry the same `X-Patchwork-Request` header and JSON content type every other write does. The count is read once when a session is confirmed, every sixty seconds while a signed-in reader has the app in front of them, and again whenever the app returns to the foreground; it is never asked for without a session and is cleared by signing out.

The patch profile's rooms add `nodes/{slug}/members` (with `after`), `nodes/{slug}/governance`, `governance/{id}`, `nodes/{slug}/governance/overview`, `nodes/{slug}/governance/record`, `nodes/{slug}/proposals` (with `status`), and `proposals/{id}`. `nodes/{slug}/events.ics` and `nodes/{slug}/events.rss` are handed to the reader's own calendar or feed app rather than fetched. A patch on a connected quilt is read from that quilt's own `nodes/{slug}`, `events`, `instance`, and `instance/icon`.

Events add one write: `POST events` with `node_id`, `title`, `starts_at` and, only where they are filled, `description`, `location`, `ends_at`, `event_url`, `image_url` and `image_alt` — trimmed, as instants, with no `timezone` (the event inherits its patch's) and no `recurrence` (the server refuses one) — and `visibility` only when the reader is posting directly. It answers 201 with the event, whose `status` is `active` or `pending_review`. The door reads `nodes/{slug}`'s `timezone`, `accept_event_suggestions`, `follower_permissions.events` and `moved_to`, the envelope's `viewer_trusted` and `is_banned`, the membership index, `auth/me`'s `role`, and `instance`'s `submissions_enabled`. A refusal is shown in the server's own words; a moved patch's 403 carries `moved_to` beside its sentence, which `APIError` keeps. A pending suggestion is readable by its submitter at `events/{id}` and is on no list, so the app lists it nowhere.

Governance adds the member's acts: `POST proposals/{id}/vote` (`{"value"}`, and the proposal read again after it, because a sole voter's ballot settles it in the request), `PUT proposals/{id}/ballot` (`{"candidate_ids","abstain"}` — the whole set each time, candidate row ids), `POST proposals/{id}/candidates` (`{"statement"}` to stand, `{"user_id"}` to put somebody forward), `DELETE proposals/{id}/candidates/me`, `GET`/`POST proposals/{id}/comments` (`{"body","parent_id"}`), `PATCH`/`DELETE comments/{id}`, `POST comments/{id}/reactions` (`{"emoji"}`) and `DELETE comments/{id}/reactions/{emoji}`, the emoji percent-encoded in the path and the heart always sent with its variation selector. `PUT` joins the verbs. The nominee picker pages `nodes/{slug}/members` by `after`. `proposals/{id}` is read with the session, which is what fills `can_vote`, `my_vote`, `approved_by_me`, `i_abstained` and every reaction's `me`; the overview's `needs_vote` is the room's count. Who may comment is the membership index plus the patch's `follower_permissions.proposals`, read from `nodes/{slug}` for a follower before a composer is drawn.

The noticeboard adds a member's reads and writes, every one of which answers 404 to anybody outside the room: `GET nodes/{slug}/notices` (`limit`, `after`; newest first, with `may_post`, `replies_default` and `notice_posting` beside the page), `POST nodes/{slug}/notices` (`{"title","body","image_url","image_alt","replies_open","tell_members"}`), `GET notices/{id}` (the notice with `node_slug`, `may_edit` and `may_manage`), `PATCH notices/{id}` — sent either as an edit (the four content fields, the author's) or as the replies switch alone (`{"replies_open"}`, the author's or a patch admin's), never both, because the server refuses the pair whole for an admin who is not the author — `DELETE notices/{id}`, `GET`/`POST notices/{id}/replies` (oldest first; `{"body"}`), `DELETE replies/{id}`, and `POST reports` (`{"entity_type":"notice"|"reply","entity_id","reason","details"}`). Lengths are checked in bytes before sending, as the server counts them.

Settings adds the account's own calls: `PATCH auth/me` with only the fields being saved (`display_name`, `bio`, `links`, `start_on_my_quilt`, `hide_amended_linings`), `GET`/`POST auth/recovery-codes` (counts, and a new batch of ten shown once), `GET auth/sessions`, `POST auth/sessions/revoke-others`, `DELETE auth/sessions/{id}`, `GET users/me/export` and `GET users/me/seamrip` (named by their `Content-Disposition` and handed to the share sheet), and `DELETE users/me` with `{"confirm_username"}`. That last one is step-up gated: a 403 whose `code` is `sudo_required` or `passkey_required` opens the Confirm sheet, which reads `GET auth/step-up` (`recovery_ready`) and spends a code with `POST auth/step-up/recovery`, then the call is made once more. A 409 names `sole_admin` (with the patches in the way) or `last_instance_admin`. `POST auth/recovery` (`{username, code}`) signs in with a code and opens the step-up window on arrival. `APIError` now keeps a refusal's `code` and `patches` beside its sentence. Start on My Quilt is read once per launch from `auth/me` and never re-asserted (web ADR 035).

Every one of the endpoints above the sign-in paragraph is a public read, and the app reads them the same way whether or not anyone is signed in: it asks for no member-only view of a patch, so a patch's own disclosure settings — `public_member_list`, `public_governance_record`, `visibility`, and the server's `published_only` / `admins_withheld` / `proposals_withheld` flags — are honoured as the server states them, and a withheld list is never rendered as an empty one. DTOs decode only fields this client uses; unknown fields are tolerated. The app's cookie jar is its own — no web view shares it, and nothing is forwarded from one quilt to another. Each selected quilt gets a new navigation subtree; old quilt content is discarded.

The client's direct visit to a selected quilt is not a cross-quilt blended read. It does not use `multi_quilt: false` as permission to blend data from other instances.

Following, joining, leaving and withdrawing are implemented natively; so are voting, an election's ballot and candidacy, and a proposal's discussion; so is a patch's noticeboard for its members — reading it, putting up and managing a notice, replying and reporting; so are posting an event to a patch and suggesting one; signing in by emailed code is implemented; reading, dismissing and clearing notifications is implemented; account settings and deletion are implemented, with recovery codes as the proof of presence; passkeys are not, because associated domains must be declared per domain ahead of time and this client connects to any quilt by address. Notification preferences, the contact card, moving, the personal calendar feed, the steward listing, invitations, the noticeboard's settings and its report queue, editing a reply, member-only content, creating or editing patches, editing or deleting events (and event links, the trust request, the CSV upload and the any-patch event form), approving the requests the Dashboard counts, proposing, withdrawing, deciding and applying proposals, and moderation are follow-on work. The web remains the explicit route to those. No disabled pretend buttons represent them, and no control appears for a reader who is not signed in.
